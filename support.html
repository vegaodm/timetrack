Track the day, one tap at a time.

Spendly Time Track turns your 24 hours into a pie chart. Tap an activity to start tracking, tap it again to stop — and run as many timers at once as you need.

Version 1.0 · Requires iOS 27 or later · iPhone and iPad

Quick start

  1. Open Records. Each tile is an activity, such as Sleep, Reading or Commute.
  2. Tap a tile to start its timer. The tile lifts into Liquid Glass and shows LIVE.
  3. Tap the same tile again to stop. The session is saved to the database immediately.
  4. Tap Stats to see where the hours went — Day, Week, Month or Year.

Timers keep running while the app is in the background or closed, because each record stores an absolute start time. Reopen the app and the elapsed time is still correct.

Timers

Run several activities at once

Start any number of timers — for example “Commute” and “Podcast” together. While timers are running, a Now tracking strip appears above your categories; tap a capsule there to stop that activity. To stop everything, open Settings and use Stop All Timers.

Tiles and list layout

Switch between the grid and list layouts with the toolbar button in Records, or in Settings → Display → Layout. Your choice is remembered.

What the cards show

  • Today ring — the share of the 24-hour day used by each category, plus the untracked remainder.
  • Tile time — how much time you have tracked for that activity today.
  • Category totals — the sum of its activities for today.

Categories and activities

A category groups related activities. For example Study can contain Deep Work, Reading and Courses. The default library is fully editable.

  1. Open Settings → Categories & Activities.
  2. Tap + to create a category: name it, pick a color and an icon.
  3. Open the category and tap + to add activities to it.
  4. Swipe a row to edit or delete.
  5. Tap Edit, then drag the handles to reorder categories, or activities inside a category. The new order is saved in the database.
  6. Archive an activity you no longer use. It disappears from Records, but its history is kept.

Deleting an activity or category also deletes its recorded time. Archive instead if you may want the history later — and export a backup before any major cleanup.

Live Activities

Starting a timer also starts a Live Activity. On the Lock Screen it shows as a card with the activity icon, its name, a summary of the other running timers and the elapsed time. In the Dynamic Island it shows as a pill with the activity icon and the live elapsed time, plus a “+N” badge when other timers are running alongside.

Expanding the island (long-press; iOS decides when the expanded view appears, apps cannot disable it) shows four corners of detail: the activity name on the left, the elapsed time on the right, when the timer started in the lower-left corner and how much of today is tracked in the lower-right corner.

The island shows hours and minutes only — the seconds are hidden so the pill fits within the width the system allows. Timers over 24 hours keep counting total hours (26 hours reads as “26:15”), so the island never has to switch formats. Tap the island or the card to open the app. The activity ends when you stop the last running timer.

With several timers running, the Live Activity headlines the one that started first and summarizes the others on the Lock Screen card (for example “Also Nap · +1”). The island can only show one headline timer; open the app to see every running timer side by side.

Statistics

Use the arrows to move to a previous or next period, and the refresh button to reload. Tap a category in Breakdown to expand its activities.

Why can tracked time exceed 24 hours?

Parallel timers overlap. The Day ring always represents the 24-hour day, so overlapping sessions are normalised in the chart while the totals report the real sum of tracked time. The app shows an explanatory note whenever this happens.

Backup and restore

Everything lives in one SQLite database file. Backups are portable copies of that file.

Export

  1. Open Settings → Backup.
  2. Tap Export Database.
  3. Choose where to send the file in the share sheet — Files, iCloud Drive, AirDrop, Mail, and so on.

Import

  1. Open Settings → Backup → Import Database.
  2. Pick a previously exported .sqlite file.
  3. Confirm in the prompt that appears in the centre of the screen. The file is checked first, then copied into place.

Import replaces all current categories, activities and records. A safety copy of the previous database is kept in the app’s Backups folder, and its filename is shown after the import.

About screen

Settings → About shows the number of records, categories and activities, the app version, and links to the privacy policy and this support page.

FAQ

Does the app sync between devices?

Not in version 1.0. Data is local to each device. Export the database on one device and import it on another to move your history.

Do I need an internet connection?

No. Tracking, statistics and backups work entirely offline. Only the Privacy and Support links need a browser.

Can I edit the start or end time of a record?

Not yet. Tap the tile to stop a timer and start again if you need a new segment.

I deleted an activity by accident. Can I get its time back?

Only from a backup. If you exported one before deleting, import it in Settings → Backup. This is why the app keeps a safety copy when you import.

Will my data survive an app update?

Yes. Updating keeps the app’s data container. Deleting the app removes it, so export a backup first if you plan to reinstall.

The numbers look different after midnight.

Records and the Day range describe the current calendar day. Sessions that cross midnight are split at midnight for day-level statistics.

Does the layout adapt to iPad and foldables?

Yes. The interface uses size classes and system bars, so it adapts to iPad multitasking, landscape, and iPhone Duo poses, including the side-mounted controls.

Why does the Dynamic Island show only hours and minutes?

The island has a limited width, so the seconds are hidden to keep the pill short. The Lock Screen card and the app still show the exact time to the second. Durations over 24 hours keep counting in total hours — 26 hours reads as “26:15”, never “1d 2h”.

Why does the island open into a large card when I press it?

That expanded view is drawn by iOS, not by the app, and Apple gives apps no way to turn it off. It appears on a long press or when the system decides to show an update. Swipe up anywhere on the screen to collapse it again.
